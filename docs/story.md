# Stacked Deck: the story

You've been hired to run the floor at **The Stacked Deck**, an esoteric
casino where every croupier knows exactly one move. You never meet the
**Owner**. Instructions arrive as notes under the cash tray, each sealed with
an ace of spades. Over five chapters the casino goes from a quiet opening night
to running the city, and each chapter's arc follows the croupiers it
introduces.

Tone: light and dry. Each level's story is one to three short paragraphs that
explain the puzzle, add a beat to the plot, and introduce any new croupier.

## Cast

* **The Owner**: unseen. Signs every note with the ace of spades, which is
  also the house's calling card. The last envelope is addressed to you.
* **Old Thursday**: a retired stage magician who has bet on hearts for forty
  years.
* **The Marchetti twins**: two tables, one desk.
* **Dmitri**: an accountant who plays exactly one hour a night and likes even
  numbers.
* **The Vellanis**: the diamond family from across the river (Sal and his
  uncle). They like red.
* **The Kessler crew**: from the coal yards. They like black, deal from the
  bottom, and are sure the house hides aces.
* **Commissioner Pryce**: from the city Gaming Commission. He takes envelopes,
  writes regulations, and likes to win on Tuesdays.
* **The mayor**: likes records.

## Chapter 1: Opening Night (passing, routing, the suit splitter)

The casino opens. Cards only need to reach the cashier: around a bar the Owner
had put in overnight, then from two tables at once. Then Old Thursday wants his
hearts paid, and you learn that a port with no direction drops cards on the
carpet. The Vellanis show up wanting red cards paid and the Kesslers want black
ones, which is the first hint that the families are circling the place.

| Level | Idea |
|---|---|
| Opening Night | pass |
| Around the Bar | routing around an obstacle (a ring-shaped floor) |
| Two Tables | two sources merging into one desk |
| Hearts Only | suit splitter; unassigned ports drop cards |
| Seeing Red | suits sharing a side; pillars on the floor |
| Black Tie | two sources plus suits on a cramped floor |

## Chapter 2: Stacking the Deck (straight, shoe, gather, cut, flush)

Business picks up and hands get bigger. The Owner's notes get more particular,
"pay the neighbours of 7", and you learn to stack a reference card from a shoe
under the player's card, test the pair, and cut the reference off again. The
Vellani uncle asks for red flushes. Dmitri's even-money bet closes the chapter
with three reference stages sharing one cut.

| Level | Idea |
|---|---|
| Back to Back | straight (two-card hands; aces high or low, no wrap) |
| Next Door | shoe + gather + straight + cut: the reference card; a narrow corridor |
| Red Flush | flush, combined with suits |
| Even Money | several reference stages merging into one shared cut |

## Chapter 3: The Regulars (n of a kind, War, duplicate, set)

Five-card hands arrive, and so does War. A rival casino opens two blocks away,
and the Owner answers with ever more generous house games. You learn that War
against a shoe compares a hand with a constant ("ace in the hole" is just "beats
a king"). Then you learn to split a hand, play its parts against each other
and put it back together. Then comes the big idea: **duplicate the hand, tear
the copy apart, and let the copy decide at a War whether the original is paid**.

| Level | Idea |
|---|---|
| Pair Night | n of a kind, chained (pair but not trips) |
| Beat the Dealer | War against a shoe |
| Ace in the Hole | high-card War on a whole hand |
| Top Dog | cut, War, gather the pieces back |
| High Spade | duplicate; copy + reference + suits plays the original at a gate |
| Top Pair | set; the same gate idea |

## Chapter 4: House Rules (top-card War, shuffles, deal)

Commissioner Pryce takes an envelope and leaves a regulation: War by the top
card only. (These levels allow only top-card War.) The Kesslers' bottom dealer
gets his own game, so you learn the riffles. Then the knockout tournament
brings the best card to the top, and Pryce's Tuesday game turns the same
tournament upside down to find the lowest card.

| Level | Idea |
|---|---|
| New Rules | top-card War |
| Bottom Dealer | in/out shuffles move the bottom card to the top |
| Knockout | deal + tournament: the best card rises to the top |
| Low Ball | the tournament reversed: the lowest card rises |

## Chapter 5: The House Always Wins (memory, gate and door, finale)

The rival casino folds, and its owner now deals for you. The regulation
quietly disappears (both War rules are allowed again). The council meets in
the back room, and the Vellanis and the Kesslers both start wearing spades in
their hatbands. "The house remembers": a shoe that deals once seeds a memory,
and a champion loops through a War. Bigger decisions need the full pattern:
test a copy, release a verdict card, gate the original, and let in **one hand
at a time** through a ticket door. The final game is a record table with the
whole city sitting at it. Afterwards an envelope with your name on it waits
under the cash tray, sealed with an ace of spades. (What's inside is left for
an epilogue: most likely, you're the Owner now.)

| Level | Idea |
|---|---|
| Hot Streak | memory: a copy of each card waits to be the next dealer; shoe `once` seeds it |
| High Roller | the champion loop |
| Full House | test a copy, release, gate, and the ticket door |
| Calling Card | sort; the door pattern again with a one-card verdict |
| House Record | finale: classify, champion, release, gate and door together |

## Notes for writers

* Keep each level's story to one to three short paragraphs. New croupiers
  are introduced in a sentence, in the story of the level that unlocks them.
* The brief is the precise rule. The story can be playful, but it must never
  contradict the brief. Edge cases (aces high, ties go to the house, two pair
  versus trips) belong in the story or the brief whenever a player could
  reasonably guess wrong.
