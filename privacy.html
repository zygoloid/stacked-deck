Privacy

Last updated 27 October 2026.

You can play Stacked Deck without an account, and then nothing about you leaves your browser. Signing in with Google is optional: it's how you agree to the game storing the things below online, and you can delete all of it from within the game at any time.

Who runs it

Stacked Deck is a free hobby game made by zygoloid, hosted on GitHub Pages. Its source code, including exactly what it stores, is on GitHub. To ask about your data, open an issue (it's public, so don't put personal details in it; say how to reach you and you'll be contacted).

Without signing in

  • Your progress (your machines, scores and which floors you've cleared) is saved in your browser's local storage, on your device only.
  • There are no cookies, analytics, ads or trackers, and the game loads nothing from other sites.
  • GitHub serves the game's pages, and like any web host it sees your IP address when it does; see GitHub's privacy statement.

When you sign in

The online features run on Google's Firebase, which stores and processes the data for the game, in the United States.

  • Your sign-in account. Google tells Firebase's sign-in service your Google account's ID, name, email address and profile picture link. The game itself uses only an account number; nobody else sees any of these.
  • A nickname you choose (not your Google name).
  • Your solutions: the machines you've cleared floors with, when you found them, and their scores, which the game's server checks.
  • Your progress, so it follows you to other devices: the machine you're working on for each floor, which floors you've cleared, and which solutions you've deleted.
  • Friends: who you are friends with, and an invite code while one of yours is open (each lasts a week at most).
  • Abuse protection. When you sign in, the game uses Google's reCAPTCHA (through Firebase App Check) to check that requests come from the real game. It collects information about your browser and device, under Google's privacy policy and terms.
  • Logs. The game's server logs its requests (with your IP address and browser), which account submitted each solution, and requests to delete data, to find faults and abuse. These logs are kept for 30 days.

Who sees what

  • Your machines are never shown to anyone else.
  • Friends (people who accepted your invite link, or whose link you accepted) see your nickname and your best scores on each floor.
  • Everyone else who signs in sees each floor's chart, which counts how many players got each score, never who.

How long it's kept, and deleting it

Everything is kept until you delete it; there are no backups.

  • Sign out from the account menu stops the game using your account in that browser, and can clear that browser's progress.
  • Delete my data, in the account menu, deletes everything above: your solutions, scores, progress, nickname, friendships (both ways) and invite, takes your scores out of the floor charts, and deletes your sign-in account. Only the logs remain, until they expire. Google may ask you to sign in again to confirm.

Your rights

Signing in is your consent to the above, and deleting your data withdraws it. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected, and to complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes

If this notice changes, the new version will be on this page, and its history is in the game's source code.
